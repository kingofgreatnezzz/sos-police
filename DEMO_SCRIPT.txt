 DEMO SCRIPT — DEFENCE DAY
 Emergency Response and SOS Location Tracking System
 A Case Study of the Nigeria Police Force, Enugu State Command
================================================================================

This file is your step-by-step runbook. Print it and keep it beside the laptop.
Total demonstration time: 15 minutes (a 7-minute cut-down version is at the end).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 SECTION 0 — THE STORY YOU ARE TELLING (read this once before the defence)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Your system now has TWO SIDES, and that is the whole point of the defence:

    SIDE 1  THE CITIZEN   — a phone app with one big red button.
                            No account, no form-filling under stress. Name,
                            phone, emergency type and GPS, then one press.

    SIDE 2  THE POLICE    — the command dashboard, which RINGS like a control
                            room telephone within 3 seconds of that press,
                            shows the citizen on the map, and has already
                            calculated which patrol unit is nearest.

    THE LINK              — both sides are the same program and the same
                            SQLite database. There is no server in between,
                            no internet service, no subscription.

The sentence to remember:

    "The citizen presses one button and the Command's phone rings — three
     seconds later the nearest patrol unit has already been calculated."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 SECTION 2 — STARTING THE SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 Double-click:   setup_and_start.bat

 The launcher will:
    1. pick your existing Python environment (it reuses ..\myenv)
    2. install anything missing
    3. build and seed data\sos_emergency.db
    4. generate the two sound files (no internet needed)
    5. start ONE server that serves BOTH modes
    6. open Tab 1 (Police Command) and Tab 2 (Citizen SOS)
    7. print the phone URL for the handset

 Opening the citizen app directly:  http://localhost:8501/?mode=citizen
 Opening the police dashboard:      http://localhost:8501/?mode=police

   Type  commander  /  npf123  and press Sign in.

   SAY: "Access is role-based. Passwords are never stored — they are hashed
         with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 120 000 iterations and a unique random salt,
         and compared in constant time to prevent timing attacks."


--- STEP 2 · Point at the mode switcher -------------------------------- 30 sec

   Point to the two buttons at the very top of the page:
       [ CITIZEN SOS ]   [ POLICE COMMAND ]

   SAY: "One program, two front ends, one database. The citizen side needs no
         login at all — a citizen in distress must never be asked for a
         password. The Command side is behind the staff sign-in."


--- STEP 3 · Show the control room is calm ----------------------------- 30 sec

   Look at the Dashboard. There is a GREEN strip:

       "Control room quiet — no unacknowledged SOS alerts."

   SAY: "The control room is quiet. The system is polling the database every
         three seconds, waiting."

   >>> This is important: it proves the alarm does NOT ring on login. It only
       rings for a genuinely new emergency. Examiners notice the difference
       between a real alerting system and one that just beeps constantly.


--- STEP 4 · GO TO THE CITIZEN'S PHONE (Tab 2, or the handset) ---------- 2 min

   Switch to Tab 2 (or pick up the phone).

   SAY: "This is what the citizen sees — no login, no menu, no jargon.
         But more importantly, look at what is NOT here. There is no form.

         A person whose shop is being robbed at gunpoint is not going to type
         their name and address. Emergency research is unambiguous on this: every
         field you require is a delay, and delay costs lives.

         So the design rule is — PRESS FIRST, DETAILS LATER."

   Point at the three one-tap categories:

       [ 🔫 Robbery / Theft ]  [ 🥊 Fight / Attack ]  [ 🚑 Health Emergency ]

   SAY: "Three broad choices instead of five, because a frightened person cannot
         classify an emergency under pressure. And none of them is compulsory —
         the button works even if you touch nothing at all."

   >>> DEMONSTRATE THE STRONGEST VERSION FIRST:
       Press the BIG RED BUTTON *immediately*, with the form completely blank.
       (Do this before typing anything — it is far more convincing.)

       SAY: "Watch. I have typed nothing. Name is empty. Phone is empty.
             I have not even chosen a category. I am just pressing the button."

   THEN show what the control room actually received (Step 6).

   >>> THEN, if you want to show the fuller version, go Back and repeat with:
        a) Tap the category:      [ 🔫 Robbery / Theft ]
        b) Tap:  "Use Simulated Enugu Location (Ogui Road area)"
        c) Optionally type your name: John Doe

   Note the coordinates change to 6.452100, 7.508900.

   SAY: "The phone would normally read its own GPS — that button is there and it
         works — but for a controlled demonstration I am placing the caller at
         Ogui Road, Enugu, with known coordinates.

         That is deliberate: those coordinates are 0.84 km from the State Police
         Headquarters, so you can verify the distance calculation against Google
         Maps yourself."

   * If you have time and the phone has GPS, add:
     "Watch what happens when I let the phone use its own GPS instead..."
     (tap '📡 Detect My GPS Location', tap ALLOW) — the page reloads once with
     the real coordinates. THEN press the simulated-location button again to put
     it back at Ogui Road for a predictable answer.


--- STEP 5 · PRESS THE BIG RED BUTTON ------------------------------ 20 sec

   PAUSE. Let them look at the button.

   SAY: "One button. Nothing else is required to save a life."

   PRESS IT.

   Tab 2 now shows the full-screen green confirmation:

       🚨  SOS SENT!
       Help is on the way. Stay calm.

   ...and the phone plays a short three-note confirmation chime.

   SAY: "The citizen gets an immediate confirmation, an incident number, and a
         live tracking screen. They are not left wondering whether it worked.

         And notice what appears BELOW the confirmation — the optional fields.
         The alert has already gone. Anyone still able to give their name and
         number can add it now, and it updates the same incident. Press first,
         details later."

   >>> Double-tap protection: a second press within 8 seconds is ignored, so a
       panicking caller cannot accidentally create two incidents and set off two
       alarms. Mention this if asked about robustness.


--- STEP 6 · THE ALARM (Tab 1) — THE WOW MOMENT ------------------------ 30 sec

   SWITCH TO TAB 1 IMMEDIATELY. Within three seconds:

       * a thick RED BORDER flashes around the whole dashboard
       * the RINGING ALARM sounds, continuously
       * a large red banner appears:

             🚨 NEW EMERGENCY! 🚨
             📞 Other · Unknown caller
             📞 Not provided | 📍 6.448300, 7.502300
             🕒 received just now | 🆔 SOS-00006

   (If you filled the form in instead, the banner shows the citizen's real name,
    phone number and the Ogui Road coordinates.)

   LET IT RING. Do not talk over it for two seconds. Let them hear it.

   SAY: "The officer did not refresh anything, did not search, did not touch
         the keyboard. The citizen pressed one button — with nothing filled in —
         and the Command's phone is ringing three seconds later, with an
         incident number, a position and a call-back requirement already logged.

         And look at the record itself: the caller is marked as 'Unknown caller'
         and the number as 'Not provided'. The system does not invent data and it
         does not refuse the call. It tells the officer exactly what it knows and
         exactly what it does not know — which is what an operational system must
         do."


--- STEP 7 · The three nearest units, already calculated ---------------- 1 min

   Still on Tab 1, directly under the banner, three cards:

       🥇 PATROL-01-OGUI        ~0.9 km   ETA ~1.5 min
       🥈 PATROL-04-INDEPENDENCE ~1.0 km  ETA ~1.7 min
       🥉 PATROL-02-ABAKPA       ~2.5 km  ETA ~4.3 min

   SAY: "Nobody calculated this. The moment the alert arrived the system
         measured the great-circle distance from the caller to EVERY patrol
         unit using the Haversine formula, discarded the ones that are already
         committed or offline, and ranked the rest nearest first.

         The red one is the unit we should send."

   THE SCIENCE — deliver this clearly, it is what earns the marks:

     "The Haversine formula is used because latitude and longitude are angles
      on a sphere, not flat grid coordinates.

          a = sin^2(delta-phi/2) + cos(phi1)*cos(phi2)*sin^2(delta-lambda/2)
          c = 2 * atan2(sqrt(a), sqrt(1-a))
          d = R * c        with R = 6371.0088 km

      I used atan2 rather than asin because it stays numerically stable for
      both very small and very large distances.

      Validated: the State Police Headquarters to Ogui Road Division computes
      as 0.843 km, which matches Google Maps to within a few metres.

      The ETA is (km / 40 km/h) * 60 * 1.15 — the 15% is an allowance for
      traffic lights, potholes and checkpoints here in Enugu."


--- STEP 8 · Mark as Dispatched — the alarm stops ----------------------- 30 sec

   Press:   [ 📲 Mark as Dispatched ]

   The ringing STOPS instantly, the red border disappears, and a green toast

       PATROL-01-OGUI dispatched to SOS-00006. Distance 0.89 km — ETA 1.5 min.

   SAY: "The system sent the NEAREST available unit automatically. The alarm

         pressed a mute button. And note that the unit has been marked as
         Dispatched in the fleet register at the same time, so it cannot be
         double-booked to another call. That is a foreign key relationship
         between the alerts table and the patrol units table, enforced by the
         database itself."

 SECTION 4 — ANSWERS TO THE QUESTIONS YOU WILL BE ASKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q: "Why is there no form on the citizen app? Don't you need the details?"
A: "Deliberately not. Every compulsory field is a delay, and in a robbery or a
    kidnapping a delay can cost a life. The design rule is press first, details
    later: the citizen presses one button and the whole incident is raised using
    the position alone, which is the only thing the Command truly needs to act.
    Name and phone are then offered as optional fields AFTER the alert is
    already with the control room. Where the caller cannot supply them, the
    record says 'Unknown caller' and 'Not provided' rather than inventing data,
    so the officer can see exactly what is and is not known."

Q: "Why only three categories? Did you not lose information?"
A: "A frightened person cannot classify an emergency under pressure, so I
    reduced five classifications to three broad taps: Robbery/Theft,
    Fight/Attack and Health Emergency. None of them is compulsory either.
    Those three map onto the five values the database accepts, and the caller's
    own wording is always preserved in the description field, so an officer can
    reclassify the incident later without losing what the caller reported."

Q: "What if the caller presses the button twice by mistake?"
A: "There is an eight-second debounce. A second press while an alert is still
    fresh returns the caller to their tracking screen instead of raising a
    second incident, so one panic cannot put two emergencies on the board and
    set off two alarms."

Q: "How does the phone talk to the dashboard? Is it a real server?"
A: "Both modes are served by one Streamlit server process from one program.
    The citizen's press writes a row into the SQLite database; the dashboard
    polls that same database every three seconds. There is no message broker
    and no internet dependency — if the Wi-Fi drops, the alarm still rings."

Q: "Is it safe for two people to write at the same time?"
A: "Yes. SQLite runs in WAL mode, which allows readers while a writer is
    active, and I set a busy timeout with automatic retries, so a collision
    waits a fraction of a second instead of failing. I tested it: 40
    simultaneous inserts from 5 concurrent connections, zero lock errors."

Q: "Why does the alarm not ring when I log in?"
A: "Because that would be useless. The monitor records the highest alert ID at
    sign-in; anything above that is a genuinely new arrival. A very recent
    alert raised within the last 5 minutes still rings, which covers the case
    where the citizen sends the SOS a moment before the officer signs in."

Q: "Where do the sounds come from? Are they copyrighted?"
A: "They are generated by the program itself, on first run, using Python's
    built-in 'wave' module — a three-note chime for the citizen and a
    telephone-style ringing alarm for the control room. Nothing is downloaded,
    so there is no copyright issue and no internet dependency."

Q: "Why is the citizen's alert status 'Active' and not 'ACTIVE'?"
A: "The database enforces a CHECK constraint listing the four permitted
    statuses — Active, Dispatched, Resolved and False Alarm — so the value
    must match exactly. That is deliberate: it makes it impossible to store a
    status the system does not understand."

Q: "What if the GPS is wrong or unavailable?"
A: "The citizen can override it: the app offers a device-GPS button, a
    simulated Enugu location button, and manual coordinate entry. A wrong
    position is still far better than no position, and the dispatcher can see
    the coordinates on the map before committing a unit."

Q: "How is this different from just calling 112?"
A: "112 gives you a voice call and the operator has to write down a landmark.
    This gives the control room a precise coordinate, a categorised emergency
    type, a named caller with a call-back number, an automatic nearest-unit
    recommendation, and a permanent audit record — in about three seconds."

Q: "How did you test it?"
A: "Three automated suites. tests/functional_test.py covers the database,
    the dispatch lifecycle, the Haversine accuracy against known distances,
    the PDF structure and concurrent writes. tests/smoke_test_ui.py renders
    every page in both roles. tests/two_mode_test.py performs the entire
    demonstration flow — citizen presses SOS, police detects and rings,
    dispatch stops the alarm. All three pass with zero failures."

Q: "What would you do next?"
A: "Release the citizen app as a real Android/iOS application with one-tap SOS
    and offline SMS fallback; integrate the 112 emergency line; add live
    traffic data for better ETAs; and give each division its own dashboard
    showing only its area of responsibility."

 IF YOU DO NOT KNOW AN ANSWER:
    "That is a good question sir. I have not implemented that, but my
     understanding is ... and I would approach it by ..."
    Never bluff. A clear "I do not know, but here is how I would find out"
    scores better than an invented answer.

 SECTION 5 — IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 THE ALARM DOES NOT RING
   > Most likely the browser is blocking audio because the page has not been
     clicked yet. Click anywhere on Tab 1 once, then have the citizen press
     the button again.
   > Check the laptop volume and that it is not muted.
   > Click "🔔 Test alarm" on the dashboard — if that is silent, it is the
     volume or the speakers, not the system.
   > LAST RESORT: click "🎭 DEMO MODE" on the dashboard. It injects the same
     kind of alert and the whole flow continues. Say honestly:
     "Let me use the built-in demo injector, which writes the identical record."

 THE PHONE CANNOT REACH THE LAPTOP
   > Both devices must be on the same Wi-Fi, and Windows Firewall must allow
     Python (see the netsh command in Section 1).
   > Use Tab 2 on the laptop instead and say: "The demonstration is identical;
     the phone and the second tab are the same client."

 THE BROWSER TAB HAS GONE BLANK OR LOST CONNECTION
   > Press F5 to reload. Streamlit reconnects automatically and the login
     session is preserved.
   > If the green "SOS COMMAND SERVER" window was closed, the server is gone:
     run setup_and_start.bat again (about 20 seconds).

 PORT 8501 ALREADY IN USE
   > The launcher switches to 8502 automatically and prints the new URLs.

 THE DATA LOOKS MESSY AFTER EXPERIMENTS
   > setup_and_start.bat reset   — the database is rebuilt and re-seeded in
     seconds, and the alarm state is cleared.

 A RED ERROR BOX APPEARS
   > It is caught by the error handler, not a crash. Note the message, click
     another menu item, and continue. Do not debug in front of the panel.

 REHEARSE THE RECOVERY ONCE BEFORE THE DAY. Confidence in the recovery is
 worth more than perfection in the first run.
